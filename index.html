A stream mix console for Linux, not a patchbay.

Split your game, voice chat, mic, music, alerts and desktop audio into named buses. Send each one to your headphones, to the stream, or to both. OBS captures Rostrum Stream Mix and Rostrum Mic separately.

Download 0.1.0 AppImage Release notes

Free and open source (Apache-2.0). 0.1.0 is available for x86_64. Validated on Ubuntu 26.04; glibc 2.43+ required. Requirements and launch.

A mixing desk, not a graph

Tools like qpwgraph and Helvum show every port and every link. Rostrum hides the graph and gives you a mixing desk instead: faders, meters, mute and solo, and scenes you can switch live.

Rostrum uses PipeWire and WirePlumber. Its AppImage was validated on Ubuntu 26.04 x86_64, not every Linux desktop. The virtual devices live in PipeWire, not in the app; applications can fall back to the system output when Rostrum quits or crashes.

Buses, not wires

Each source gets a named bus with a fader and a meter. No ports to connect by hand.

Two mixes from one console

What you hear and what your audience hears, set per bus: Headphones, Stream or Both.

Features

Everything below is in version 0.1.0.

  • Six buses to start

    Mic, Game, Voice, Music, Alerts and Desktop, each with a fader and mute. Every playback bus gets solo. Add more, up to twelve.

  • Headphones and Stream mixes

    Send each bus to your headphones, the stream, or both. Headphones Only removes Stream Mix sends; Stream Only removes headphone sends. Independent OBS captures can bypass these exclusions.

  • Your mic, protected

    The mic has its own bus with optional sidetone. If a saved mic disappears, the stream mic stays silent until it returns. No other mic replaces it unless you explicitly enable fallback.

  • Local microphone filters

    RNNoise reduces background noise, alongside a rumble filter, gate, EQ, compressor and limiter. Choose the stream and eligible recording apps, or only the stream, with per-app choices. Audio tools stay plain by default. Processing runs inside PipeWire.

  • Check your stream mic

    Check Mic records five seconds of Rostrum Mic, with its gain and filters, and plays it back in your headphones only. It reports the mic level before OBS; it does not verify audience audio.

  • Apps find their bus

    Discord goes to Voice, Spotify to Music, Steam and Proton games to Game, Streamer.bot to Alerts. Every placement shows why it was made.

  • Apps remember their bus

    Save an app’s bus with “Always” and it lands there again, including before Rostrum starts at your next login. If Easy Effects pulls an app away as it starts, Rostrum moves it back.

  • Per-app volume and mute

    Turn one app down or mute it without touching the rest of its bus. Every app shows its own icon, in the mixer too.

  • Balance and mono headphones

    Pan any playback bus left or right, and fold your headphones to mono if you listen with one ear.

  • Scenes

    Recall every level, mute and destination at once. Changes save to the live scene by themselves. Start from presets like Gaming, Just Chatting or Podcast.

  • OBS in one click

    OBS captures Rostrum Stream Mix and Rostrum Mic separately. Review the setup preview, including duplicate-capture mutes, and undo applied changes. Additional app or headphone captures can bypass exclusions.

  • Check stream readiness

    Inspect effective controls, devices, channel links and OBS capture observations. Results distinguish Verified, Needs attention, Intentionally excluded/idle and Not verified. No settings are changed and no sound is played or recorded. It cannot guarantee recording or audience audio.

  • Knows when you are live

    LIVE and REC badges follow OBS. A warning appears if you go live with your mic muted or nothing reaching the stream. OBS scenes can switch Rostrum scenes.

  • Global hotkeys

    Mute the mic or the stream and switch scenes from anywhere. Bind push to talk, push to mute and a panic mute for mic and stream at once.

  • Command line and D-Bus

    Drive Rostrum from Stream Deck buttons, KDE Connect or scripts with the rostrum command or the session bus. There is no network listener.

  • Auto-ducking and fades

    Optionally let your voice turn Music down while you speak, and fade between scenes instead of switching at once.

  • Compact view

    A small mixer with your mic, scene, masters and a slim fader per bus that can stay on top of a game or OBS.

  • Undo and backups

    Undo and redo level and bus edits. Deleted scenes wait 30 days in Recently Deleted, and one file backs up all your settings and scenes.

  • Lives in the tray

    Mute, switch scenes or restart Rostrum from the tray. Choose whether closing or minimizing the window hides it there.

A closer look

Install

Download the x86_64 AppImage

Rostrum 0.1.0 AppImage · SHA256SUMS · Release notes. Save the AppImage and checksum file in the same folder, then run: 

sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS
chmod +x Rostrum-0.1.0-x86_64.AppImage
./Rostrum-0.1.0-x86_64.AppImage
# If FUSE is unavailable:
./Rostrum-0.1.0-x86_64.AppImage --appimage-extract-and-run

Fully quit an existing Rostrum through its tray/menu Quit first; closing the window can leave it running. Builds share settings and a second launch can activate the old copy. Follow first-time setup through Create Mix.

Tested environment and requirements

The AppImage was validated in clean Ubuntu 26.04 x86_64, glibc 2.43, PipeWire 1.6.2 and WirePlumber 0.5.13, without host Qt/KDE/QML or development packages. It requires glibc 2.43 or newer; that binary floor does not establish support for every newer distribution. Older glibc and other architectures are unsupported by this artifact. A working audio session, desktop D-Bus, X11 or Wayland, graphics/font libraries and FUSE for mounted launch are required. See the host runtime packages.

Connect OBS

Open Rostrum’s OBS page and choose Set Up OBS, review the preview and apply the changes. With OBS running, enable its local obs-websocket server if needed. For manual setup, use Audio Output Capture (or the PipeWire capture plugin) for Rostrum Stream Mix (rostrum.stream.monitor with PulseAudio capture), and Mic/Aux or one Audio Input Capture for Rostrum Mic (rostrum.mic).

Use one capture per output. Disable or mute duplicate desktop, headphone/default-monitor, raw-mic and app captures: they can double audio or bypass Headphones Only exclusions. A single Rostrum bus is not the Stream Mix. Run Check stream readiness, review OBS track assignments and make a short recording; readiness cannot verify audience sound.

Build from source

Source-build instructions cover Ubuntu, Fedora and Arch. Source minimums are PipeWire 1.0+, WirePlumber 0.5+, Qt 6.5+ and KDE Frameworks 6.8+; mic filters need PipeWire 1.4+ audio-convert support. These are not the AppImage’s validated compatibility matrix. Source-build CI passes on Fedora and Arch do not validate this AppImage on those systems.

Private by default

Rostrum has no telemetry or accounts and does not upload your audio. Mic filters and the mic check run locally without saving recordings. Optional update checks and AppImage downloads contact the release servers; optional crash reports go to Sentry. OBS communication stays local.

  • Optional checks and downloads Update checks contact getrostrum.dev and GitHub without cookies or a user ID. AppImages can automatically download and install checksum-verified updates, or wait for you to choose Install. Checks and automatic installation have separate switches in Settings → Updates.
  • Crash reports are your call Official builds include optional crash reports to Sentry. By default Rostrum asks after a crash; you can inspect the report before sending, or choose Always or Never. No names, paths, device names or user IDs. Ordinary source builds omit crash reporting.
  • OBS stays on your computer Rostrum reads OBS status and capture settings over localhost. Set Up OBS and Undo apply only when you choose them. Background following can be disabled in Settings → OBS.
  • No network listener Remote control goes through the command line or the D-Bus session bus, never a port.
